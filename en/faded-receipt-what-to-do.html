Faded receipt: what to do

You dig out the receipt for the broken device and see nothing but grey streaks. It happens a lot, because many receipts are printed on thermal paper. Here is what to do now and how to avoid it next time.

Why receipts fade

Thermal paper turns dark under heat and needs no ink, which also makes it sensitive to warmth, light and some substances such as plasticisers in plastic sleeves or hand cream. The print fades or the whole receipt turns dark. The longer it sits in a wallet, a car or on a windowsill, the more likely it becomes unreadable.

Right now: secure what is still readable

  1. Photograph the receipt in good, angled light. Print sometimes shows better in a photo than to the naked eye. A scan with higher contrast can help too.
  2. Write down what you know at once: retailer, branch, approximate date, item, price, payment method.
  3. Then store the receipt dark and cool, ideally in paper rather than a plastic sleeve.

Find substitute proof

The till receipt is not always the only record. Check:

  • Bank or credit card statement: shows date, retailer and amount.
  • Customer account or loyalty card at the retailer: some keep a purchase history or offer digital receipts.
  • Email confirmation or PDF invoice for online purchases.
  • Warranty card, packaging, serial number: they help identify the device.
  • Payment app or wallet: the history often shows date and retailer.

Whether such a substitute is enough in your case is for the retailer, manufacturer or insurer to decide. Ask them before giving up; many are accommodating. We cannot give legal advice.

Prevent it next time

  • Photograph every receipt on the day of purchase.
  • For expensive items, ask for a digital receipt or an invoice by email.
  • Do not keep receipts in the car, in a trouser pocket or in sleeves containing plasticisers.

Frequently asked questions

Is a faded receipt still valid?

There is no blanket answer. What matters is whether the retailer, manufacturer or insurer can trace the purchase from your documents. Ask them and present substitute proof.

Can I make a faded receipt readable again?

Sometimes a photo or scan with raised contrast, or angled light, helps. There is no guarantee. Secure whatever is still visible straight away.

Can the retailer issue a duplicate?

That is at the retailer's discretion and depends on their till system. A loyalty card, bank statement or payment record improves your chances. No entitlement follows from it.
